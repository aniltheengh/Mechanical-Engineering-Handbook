# A practical route through the books

Start with **[units and dimensional analysis](chapters/01-units-and-dimensional-analysis.md), Example 1**. Recalculate the pump power using a calculator and write down every unit conversion. This is the first action; the full route below is a reference, not a requirement to complete many tasks at once.

## Use one chapter at a time

Read the learning map and theory. Sketch the physical system before looking at the formula list. Reproduce the derivation without copying it line by line. Cover the worked-example answer, calculate it yourself, and compare units and assumptions as well as numbers. Solve the three exercises before opening the solutions. Finally, explain one common mistake in your own words.

Keep a notebook with four columns: model, assumptions, calculation, and evidence. A wrong result with a clear explanation teaches more than an unexplained correct number. Mark a chapter “practised” only after solving its exercises independently; automated script success is not a record of your personal understanding.

## Recommended sequence

| Stage | Chapters | Practical evidence to produce |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Calculation language | 01–03 | A unit-consistent calculation and a time-step convergence table |
| 2. Forces and motion | 04–06 | A free-body diagram, support reactions, bending stress, and deflection |
| 3. Materials and mechanisms | 07, 18–20, 34 | Explain a material choice, transmission ratio, resonance risk, and lubrication regime |
| 4. Energy and fluid systems | 08–11, 14 | Close a control-volume balance and determine a pump duty |
| 5. Thermal equipment | 12, 13, 15–17 | Size an ideal heat-transfer area and distinguish sensible, latent, and chemical energy |
| 6. Making and measuring | 21–23 | A dimensioned part drawing, process route, and uncertainty/tolerance budget |
| 7. Computation | 24, 25, 30 | A simple analytical benchmark, a mesh/step study, and an active design constraint |
| 8. Electrical and control | 26–29 | A sensor scaling calculation and a feedback or robot-kinematics model |
| 9. Engineering decisions | 31–33, 35 | A reliability model, discounted cost comparison, energy boundary, and testable requirements |
| 10. Integration | [Capstone](CAPSTONE.md) | A traceable calculation package with limitations and verification criteria |
| 11. Specialist foundations | 36–41 | A crack-assessment model, composite stiffness bound, choked-flow calculation, sound-level sum, corrosion-loss estimate, and welding heat/load calculation |

The [formula handbook](books/09-formula-handbook.md) is useful for revision after studying the corresponding theory. Before using a formula, identify the physical quantity, sign convention, units, assumptions, and limiting case. If any are unclear, return to the chapter.

Before chapters 36–37, study stress/strain and material behaviour in 06–07. Before 38, study thermodynamics and fluid mechanics in 08 and 10. Vibration and wave concepts support 39. Material behaviour, chemistry, heat transfer, manufacturing, and stress analysis support 40–41. The specialist chapters make modelling boundaries explicit; a worked introductory calculation is not an integrity or fabrication acceptance procedure.

## For a plant/piping designer

After the foundations, prioritize statics, strength of materials, fluid mechanics, pipe flow and pumps, heat transfer, materials, metrology, CAD/drawing, reliability, and systems engineering. Connect a 3D model to the P&ID, line conditions, supports, access, and maintenance requirements. A geometrically correct CAD model does not establish pressure adequacy, flexibility, stress compliance, or equipment performance.

## For deeper textbook study

Choose one full text from the [open library](references/OPEN_LIBRARY.md) for the subject currently being studied. Work through its sequence rather than collecting many PDFs without solving problems. Use this handbook as a compact cross-subject index and calculation companion. External course syllabi and book contents determine their actual scope.
